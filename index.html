Core Curriculum

Fiduciary Foundations

An eight-module sequence in the disciplines that underlie every administrative and fiduciary system.

Module 01

The Record

What a record is, why institutions depend on records, and how a record is created, maintained, authenticated, and preserved.

Module 02

Parties and Capacity

Identifying persons, entities, representatives, and fiduciaries, and understanding the capacity in which each party acts.

Module 03

Standing and Jurisdiction

How authority to decide and authority to be heard are established, examined, and preserved on the record.

Module 04

Evidence and Burdens

Distinguishing facts, allegations, and conclusions, and analyzing who must prove what, by what standard, and with what evidence.

Module 05

Administrative Procedure

Notice, timing, deadlines, filings, and review — the procedural sequence through which administrative decisions are made and challenged.

Module 06

Fiduciary Duty

The duties of loyalty, care, accounting, and disclosure that bind trustees, agents, and other stewards of another's interests.

Module 07

Reading Law and Policy

A disciplined method for reading statutes, regulations, rules, and policies: definitions, elements, exceptions, and cross-references.

Module 08

Building the Administrative Record

Assembling correspondence, notices, exhibits, and procedural history into a coherent record capable of supporting review.

Learning Outcomes

Develop a repeatable method for administrative analysis.

Foundational study is designed to strengthen the student’s ability to examine records, identify authority, organize evidence, follow procedure, and preserve an accurate administrative history.

Analytical Framework

The Kelly Legacy Method

A six-part research and administrative framework applied throughout the Institute's instruction and publications.

  1. I

    Facts

    Determine what the verified record establishes.

  2. II

    Parties

    Identify each person, entity, fiduciary, representative, beneficiary, agency, and decision-maker.

  3. III

    Elements

    Identify the statutory, contractual, administrative, or procedural requirements.

  4. IV

    Evidence

    Determine whether competent evidence supports each required element.

  5. V

    Procedure

    Analyze jurisdiction, posture, notice, timing, deadlines, preservation requirements, and available motions or review.

  6. VI

    Remedy

    Identify the relief supported by law, equity, contract, policy, or the administrative record.
